United States Cars: What's Changed in 25 Years?

The automobile industry has changed a lot over the past 25 years. From changes required by the United States government, to trends and interests set by the consumer, the car market at changed a lot. Looking at data from the U.S. Department of energy, it is easy to look at all the new models offered by manufacturers to see what changes are occuring. Let's start with the government angle and the mandates pushed from them, while coinciding with changes and advances in technology: Fuel efficiency. In 2007, President George W. Bush signed the Energy and Independence and Secuirty act, which mandated that car manufactures increase the efficiency of their vehicles they created, aiming for vehicles to have an average miles per gallon (MPG) by 2020.

A major factor in driving MPG is the type of vehicle driven. In addition to the U.S. government mandating an increase in the MPG of vehicles driven, customers also get a say in what vehicles get made by voting with their wallet. Customers taste is also fickle, and can change rapidly from year to year. With that factor, if customers start to favor vehicles that historically do not get good gas mileage (Trucks, Large SUVs, etc.), that will also lead to less average MPG for vehicles customers are driving.
